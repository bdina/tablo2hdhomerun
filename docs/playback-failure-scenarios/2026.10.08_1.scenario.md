## 2. Executive Summary of Incident & Inter-Failure Period

Following the earlier streaming failure documented in [`2026.10.08_0.scenario.md`](file:///home/bdina/git/tablo2hdhomerun/docs/playback-failure-scenarios/2026.10.08_0.scenario.md) (which concluded at `13:05:17 UTC` / `09:05:17 EDT`), the system underwent an idle period followed by an extended **2 hour 22 minute** live streaming session on CBS Channel 4.1:

1. **Inter-Failure Idle Period (`13:05:17` – `13:42:20 UTC` / `09:05:17` – `09:42:20 EDT`, 37m 3s)**:
   - Tuner state was completely idle (`occupied=0`, active clients `0`).
   - The previous lease `552a0ffa` was cleanly torn down; no network traffic or requests were exchanged between the proxy and Tablo.
2. **Extended Playback Session (`13:42:20` – `16:04:50 UTC` / `09:42:20` – `12:04:50 EDT`, 142m 30s)**:
   - Streamed for **2 hours 22 minutes 30 seconds** under lease `7fadd9cd`.
   - Endured **396 tuning cycles** (1 initial tune + **395 recovery retunes**, averaging one every ~21.6 seconds).
   - Produced over **7,100 error logs** in Plex Media Server before finally terminating when the transcoder crashed (`Transcode runner appears to have died`) and the client socket hit `Broken pipe`.

### Good vs. Bad Experience Contrast
This session provides an empirical comparison of what constitutes a "good playback" experience versus an "unplayable failure" despite identical underlying retuning cycles:

- **The "Good Playback" Phase (First ~63 minutes, `09:42` – `10:45 EDT`)**:
  - The viewer experienced continuous, watchable video with only small, occasional hiccups (brief macroblock glitches or minor audio blips) that did not halt playback.
  - Plex recorded only **115 total decoder errors across 63 minutes** (~1.8 errors/min). In fact, during a 35-minute stretch (`10:10` – `10:45 EDT`), Plex logged only **6 total decoder errors**.
  - **Why it succeeded**: The proxy's MPEG-TS null packet padding kept the TCP socket alive during retunes, and newly tuned segments happened to align close to keyframe (I-frame) boundaries, allowing the Intel VAAPI transcoder and DASH player buffer to absorb discontinuities seamlessly.
- **The "Degrading / Bad" Phase (Next ~55 minutes, `10:50` – `11:45 EDT`)**:
  - Compounding retunes increasingly truncated Group of Pictures (GOP) structures mid-sequence.
  - Plex decoder errors surged to **~4,400 errors** (~80 errors/min).
  - The viewer experienced frequent macroblock tiling (`ac-tex damaged`), missing motion vectors (`00 motion_type`), and stuttering video.
- **The Terminal Failure Phase (Final ~10 minutes, `11:55` – `12:04 EDT`)**:
  - Saturated by **>2,500 decoder syntax errors**, the FFmpeg transcode pipeline suffered severe desynchronization.
  - At `12:03:35 EDT`, a rapid burst of 11 `Missing picture start code` errors occurred, triggering `write: Broken pipe` and `Transcode runner appears to have died`.
  - The live TV transcode pipeline completely collapsed, terminating the stream.

## 4. Phase-by-Phase Comparative Analysis: Good vs. Bad Experience

The 142-minute session provides an empirical baseline contrasting how identical proxy retunes manifest as completely different viewer experiences across three distinct operational phases:

```
[09:42 EDT] ========================================> [10:45 EDT] ==============================> [11:45 EDT] ===============> [12:03:35 EDT]
             Phase 1: "Good Playback"                              Phase 2: Escalating Degradation                 Phase 3: Collapse
             • 115 decoder errors in 63 min                        • 4,400 decoder errors in 55 min                • 2,500 errors in 8 min
             • Minor macroblock glitches                           • Noticeable stutter & visual tiling            • Transcode runner died
             • 100% uninterrupted streaming                        • Stream survives via null padding              • Broken pipe / crash
```

### 4.1 Phase 1: High Tolerance & "Good Playback" (`09:42` – `10:45 EDT`, ~63 Minutes)
- **Error Frequency**: Only 115 total errors recorded across 63 minutes (~1.8 errors/minute).
- **Peak Quiet Window**: Between `10:10` and `10:45 EDT` (35 continuous minutes), PMS logged only **6 total decoder errors**.
- **Viewer Perception**: Smooth, high-definition broadcast video. Occasionally, a single video frame showed slight macroblock tiling or a momentary audio blip lasting <100 ms, after which playback continued completely uninterrupted.
- **Why it worked**:
  1. **Null-Packet Padding**: The proxy's `.keepAlive(nullPacketIntervalMs.millis, () => GapFill)` continuously pushed `MPEGTS_NULL_PACKET` (PID `0x1FFF`) during the 1.0–1.2s retune window. This prevented TCP socket starvation and kept Plex's demuxer from timing out.
  2. **Clean Keyframe Alignment**: When the proxy retuned, Tablo's live encoder provided segments that frequently began with an MPEG-2 sequence header and I-frame. Combined with the injected PAT/PMT sync prefix, FFmpeg easily acquired picture synchronization without corrupting reference buffers.
  3. **Player Buffer Absorption**: The client's DASH player maintained a 6–8 second playout buffer, which absorbed the micro-stalls and retune latency without causing player underrun spinners.

### 4.2 Phase 2: Compounding Degradation & Visual Artifacts (`10:50` – `11:45 EDT`, ~55 Minutes)
- **Error Frequency**: Surged to **~4,400 errors** (~80 errors/minute).
- **Observed Glitches**: Heavy bursts of `00 motion_type` (missing motion vectors), `ac-tex damaged` (corrupted discrete cosine transform coefficients), and `slice mismatch`.
- **Viewer Perception**: The stream developed periodic video stutter, visible macroblocking pixelation squares across active motion scenes, and momentary picture freezes every ~20 seconds. However, audio remained largely intelligible, and the stream did not fail terminally.
- **Why it degraded**:
  1. **Mid-GOP Discontinuities**: As the Tablo's segment publication timing drifted relative to the proxy's retune clock, retunes began slicing directly through P-frames and B-frames in active GOPs.
  2. **Corrupted Motion Vectors**: When a P-frame references an intra-frame (I-frame) that was skipped during a retune, FFmpeg is forced to guess or skip damaged macroblocks (`skip with previntra`), causing smeared pixels on screen until the next keyframe arrives.

### 4.3 Phase 3: Terminal Collapse & Transcode Runner Death (`11:55` – `12:04 EDT`, ~10 Minutes)
- **Error Frequency**: Saturated by **>2,500 errors** within 8 minutes (>300 errors/minute).
- **The Failure Event**:
  1. At `12:03:35.563 EDT`, an extreme burst of 11 consecutive `Missing picture start code` errors occurred within 40 ms.
  2. The FFmpeg demuxer buffer experienced irrecoverable stream desynchronization.
  3. At `12:03:35.741 EDT`, client chunk delivery failed with `write: Broken pipe [system:32]`.
  4. At `12:03:35.988 EDT`, Plex Media Server declared:
     ```text
     WARN - [Req#9b4f5/Transcode/e90214ugp9d7z6246fwzuqn0] Transcode runner appears to have died.
     ```
  5. The transcode runner process exited abnormally, severing the live TV session and causing the client player to freeze permanently.

## 6. Root Cause Technical Deep Dive

### 6.1 Why Phase 1 Maintained "Good Playback"
The resilience demonstrated during the first 63 minutes was made possible by three mechanisms in [`ResilientHlsSource.scala`](file:///home/bdina/git/tablo2hdhomerun/src/main/scala/app/stream/ResilientHlsSource.scala) ([commit 9a4e105](file:///home/bdina/git/tablo2hdhomerun/src/main/scala/app/stream/ResilientHlsSource.scala)):

1. **Gap Fill via Null Packet Insertion**:
   ```scala
   .keepAlive(nullPacketIntervalMs.millis, () => GapFill)
   .via(RecoveryTimeout.flow(recoveryTimeout, streamName))
   .map {
     case Real(data) => data
     case GapFill => MPEGTS_NULL_PACKET
   }
   ```
   When the watchdog tripped and executed a cold retune, the Tablo took ~1.0–1.2 seconds to return the new playlist. During this gap, the proxy emitted standard MPEG-TS null packets (0x1FFF). Downstream, Plex's socket remained fully satisfied, preventing demuxer read timeouts.
2. **PAT/PMT and Discontinuity Prefix Injection**:
   ```scala
   val prefix = resumePrefixSupplier().getOrElse(MPEGTS_DISCONTINUITY_PACKET)
   inner.statefulMapConcat { () =>
     var isFirst = true
     chunk =>
       if (isFirst) {
         isFirst = false
         List(Real(prefix ++ chunk))
       } else {
         List(Real(chunk))
       }
   }
   ```
   Prepending the discontinuity packet and fresh PAT/PMT tables signaled to the hardware decoder that a PID timestamp reset had occurred, allowing it to discard stale reference state rather than corrupting every frame.
3. **Preserved Sequence State Across Retunes**:
   Commit `9a4e105` ensured that `lastSeqRef` was preserved across internal retunes, preventing `HlsPlaylistPoller` from re-downloading stale segments that were already sent to Plex.

### 6.2 Why the Stream Eventually Collapsed in Phase 3
Despite the recovery mechanisms, the high-frequency retune loop (~1 retune every 21 seconds) carries an inherent flaw:

1. **GOP Fragmentation**: Live broadcast TV typically uses a GOP length of 15 to 30 frames (0.5 to 1.0 second). In an HLS segment of 2.5 seconds, there are 2 to 3 GOPs. Slicing through the stream every 21 seconds has an ~80% statistical probability of landing inside an active P/B frame sequence rather than at an IDR/I-frame boundary.
2. **Buffer Desynchronization in FFmpeg**: Each discontinuity requires FFmpeg to drain its internal bitstream filter and video parsing queues. Slicing the input 395 times forced FFmpeg to repeatedly re-initialize its parser state.
3. **Transcode Runner Death**: After 2 hours and 22 minutes of continuous GOP desynchronization and >7,100 syntax errors, FFmpeg hit an unhandled packet alignment condition (`Missing picture start code` across all slices), causing the transcoder binary process to abort (`Transcode runner appears to have died`).

### 6.3 Grace Period Termination Mechanics
As in previous incidents, the proxy handled client termination cleanly:
1. When the transcode runner died at `12:03:35 EDT` (`16:03:35 UTC`), Plex severed the HTTP connection to `/auto/v4.1`.
2. `tablo2hdhomerun` detected that active clients dropped to `0` at `16:03:35.435 UTC` and engaged its **75-second idle grace timer**.
3. During the 75-second drain, the proxy completed recovery retunes 394, 395, and 396.
4. Exactly 75.000 seconds after client disconnect, at `16:04:50.435 UTC`, `[session] grace-expired` triggered, cleanly tearing down lease `7fadd9cd` and removing the session from `SessionManager`.

## 7. Architecture & Design Decisions: Informed by Good vs. Bad Experience

The empirical contrast between Phase 1 (good playback) and Phases 2 & 3 (degradation and crash) directly informs the design and architecture of `tablo2hdhomerun`:

### 1. Primary Mandate: Remove Chunk-Level Micro-Stall Trips on HLS Streams
- **The Core Finding**: The entire cycle of 395 retunes was 100% self-inflicted by `StallWatchdog` counting normal 2–3s HLS segment pacing as micro-stalls.
- **The Fix**: Remove `microStallGap` checks from `ResilientHlsSource` when operating in HLS mode. The HLS poller already tracks segment availability against `#EXT-X-TARGETDURATION`. A stream should only be declared stalled if the **playlist fails to update for $\ge 8$ seconds**, not because of inter-chunk byte arrival pauses.

### 2. Retain & Enhance MPEG-TS Null Packet Padding
- **The Success**: The null packet generator (`.keepAlive(..., () => GapFill)`) was the single mechanism that allowed playback to survive for 2 hours and 22 minutes despite 395 retunes.
- **Enhancement**: Maintain null packet padding during any upstream delay (playlist discovery, socket reconnects, or RF recovery), ensuring downstream players (Plex, VLC, Channels) never see an empty TCP pipe.

### 3. Keyframe (I-Frame) Alignment Before Discontinuity Injection
- **The Insight**: Phase 1 was smooth because retunes happened to start near I-frames, whereas Phase 2 degraded because discontinuities cut mid-GOP.
- **The Decision**: When recovering from an interruption, the proxy should parse MPEG-TS packet headers for the **Random Access Indicator (RAI)** or MPEG-2 sequence header (`0x000001B3` / I-frame slice `0x00000100`). Continue padding with null packets until the start of a clean GOP is found before asserting the `MPEGTS_DISCONTINUITY_PACKET` and PAT/PMT headers downstream.

### 4. Circuit Breaker on Consecutive Retunes
- **The Insight**: A stream that retunes every 21 seconds eventually destabilizes FFmpeg's decoder queues, causing a terminal crash after ~2 hours.
- **The Decision**: Implement an exponential backoff circuit breaker. If more than 3 retunes occur within 60 seconds, throttle retune attempts and hold padding to give upstream decoders and hardware tuners a stable operating state.
