## 2. Executive Summary of Incident

On the afternoon of **`2026-10-18`**, multiple viewing sessions on CBS Channel 4.1 (`S20431_004_01`) were conducted between **`13:28:07 EDT`** and **`14:47:14 EDT`** (`17:28:07 UTC` – `18:47:14 UTC`). Over this 1-hour 19-minute window, playback on **Plex on Apple TV 4K failed repeatedly**, suffering visual freezes, buffer starvation, and involuntary ejections back to the Plex menu. 

Simultaneously, a comparative control experiment was conducted where the user watched CBS 4.1 through the **official Tablo native application** around the same time as Plex specifically to compare real-time performance and resilience under identical broadcast conditions. While the Tablo native client experienced a few freezes with data loss when the ATSC demodulator struggled, it **always recovered and continued playing without dropping the channel, failing the player, or kicking the user back to the guide**. This empirical contrast establishes the benchmark experience desired for Plex.

Surface meteorological observations from **KMHT** confirm exceptionally stable, tranquil atmospheric conditions throughout the afternoon: temperatures of 55°F–57°F, light winds of 0–4 KT (0–4.6 mph), **0.0 mph wind gusts**, and completely clear skies (`CLR`). This rules out aerodynamic antenna mast sway or dynamic foliage multipath as the trigger, isolating the issue to **transient ATSC demodulation dropouts compounded by the fragility of the downstream Plex Live TV streaming architecture**.

### Key Architectural Findings:
1. **The Core Architectural Contrast: Plex Multi-Tier Brittleness vs. Tablo HLS Resilience**:
   - **Tablo Native Client**: Streams directly via HLS. Its media player engine expects broadcast packet drops. When packets are lost or corrupted, the player drops damaged macroblocks, pauses rendering briefly during data loss, and resumes on the next valid I-frame/IDR slice without tearing down the player session or pipeline.
   - **Plex Media Server Pipeline**: Relies on a rigid multi-tier pipeline:
     1. `tablo2hdhomerun` emulates an HDHomeRun tuner, sending continuous MPEG-TS over an HTTP socket.
     2. Plex Ingest demuxes the socket (`-codec copy`) to build a live buffer in `/transcode`.
     3. Plex Transcoder runs FFmpeg VAAPI hardware transcoding to deliver HLS/DASH fragments to Apple TV.
     4. Apple TV's native player consumes these fragments.
     When ATSC demodulator corruption occurs, FFmpeg logs thousands of decode errors (`slice mismatch`, `invalid cbp`, `00 motion_type`, `slice below image`). The transcode rate drops below 1.0x, the live ingest stream stalls, and the Apple TV player exhausts its jitter buffer. Instead of holding the stream and waiting for the next clean keyframe, the Apple TV Plex client terminates playback and dumps the user back to the menu.
2. **Tablo Hardware 8-Second Stalls Under Demodulator Stress**:
   During the afternoon window, the Tablo 4th Gen hardware tuner experienced **three separate 8-second data generation stalls**:
   - `17:35:48 UTC` (`13:35:48 EDT`) on lease `590a23b4`
   - `17:47:09 UTC` (`13:47:09 EDT`) on lease `2326b905`
   - `18:37:21 UTC` (`14:37:21 EDT`) on lease `69cee2fc`
   In all three cases, `tablo2hdhomerun`'s watchdog caught the stall (`WARN [4thgen-channel-S20431_004_01] no new data for 8s, retuning`) and initiated recovery. However, in two of the three cases, the Apple TV client had *already failed and disconnected seconds before the watchdog tripped*, because the preceding corrupted packets broke the downstream transcode pipeline.
3. **Massive Silent Decode Error Floods (Failure Mode E)**:
   Plex Media Server logged a staggering **7,821 `mpeg2video` decoding errors** across the afternoon sessions:
   - Episode 1 (`42b43365`): **1,683 errors**
   - Episode 2 (`e0a065aa`): **196 errors**
   - Episode 3A (`179528df`): **756 errors**
   - Episode 3B (`5b4e887d`): **5,186 errors** (concentrated at `14:37:13 EDT`)
   These errors confirm that the Tablo tuner was generating valid-length HLS transport stream chunks that were filled with corrupted macroblocks and invalid syntax. Because data was flowing, the zero-byte watchdog did not trip until the hardware tuner completely froze for 8 seconds.
4. **Successful Fast-Attach Hand-Off in Episode 3**:
   At `14:35:09 EDT`, when Apple TV disconnected, the proxy engaged its 180s idle-grace timer ([`Tablo4thGen.scala:L1156`](file:///home/bdina/git/tablo2hdhomerun/src/main/scala/app/tuner/Tablo4thGen.scala#L1156)). When the user re-tuned **33 seconds later** (`14:35:42 EDT`), the proxy cancelled idle grace and fast-attached client `38f6292a` to active lease `69cee2fc` without touching the Tablo hardware.

## 6. Root Cause Technical Deep Dive

### 1. Anatomy of the Downstream Pipeline Collapse on Apple TV
Why did the official Tablo native client survive the exact same broadcast impairments while Plex on Apple TV 4K collapsed?
- **Plex Ingest & Transcoder Vulnerability to Bitstream Corruption**:
  In standard HDHomeRun tuner streaming, Plex opens an HTTP socket to `/auto/v<channel>`. An ingest worker processes the raw TS stream, attempting to demux audio and video packets and write HLS fragments for the downstream client transcoder.
  When the Tablo demodulator suffers packet loss, the transport stream contains missing TS packets, broken Adaptation Fields, and mangled slice data (`slice mismatch`, `invalid cbp -1`, `00 motion_type`).
  While Intel VAAPI hardware decoding (`i965`/`iHD`) prevents FFmpeg from crashing with a segmentation fault, the corrupted macroblocks cause FFmpeg to lose reference frame state. When B-frames and P-frames reference non-existent I-frames, FFmpeg drops output frames. The output frame rate collapses from 60 fps to 0 fps.
- **Client Buffer Starvation and Ejection**:
  The Apple TV Plex client receives an HLS/DASH manifest whose published segments are delayed or missing. The Apple TV player engine buffers for a few seconds. If fresh valid video chunks are not delivered within the player's timeout window, the client issues a stop command and terminates the session.
  Crucially, this means **the client disconnects while `tablo2hdhomerun` is still waiting for upstream data**. Seconds later, the proxy's 8-second watchdog trips, but by that point, the downstream client is already gone.

### 2. The Resilient Tablo Native Client Pattern
The official Tablo client utilizes a native HLS consumption model specifically engineered for OTA broadcast realities:
- **Tolerant GOP Drop Strategy**: When an HLS segment is corrupted or missing, the player does not attempt to decode broken P-frames without reference headers. It drops the damaged GOP entirely.
- **Persistent Player Session State**: The player enters a brief pause/freeze state, holding the last coherent frame on screen. It does not treat a 4- to 8-second data pause as a fatal error.
- **Immediate IDR Keyframe Resynchronization**: As soon as the Tablo tuner recovers and emits a segment with a valid IDR/I-frame, the native player locks onto the new PTS timestamp and resumes audio/video playback seamlessly.
This establishes the architectural benchmark: **a broadcast-grade proxy must emulate this resiliency toward Plex**.

## 7. Architecture & Design Recommendations

To achieve the native Tablo client's resilience within the Plex ecosystem, `tablo2hdhomerun` must shield Plex Media Server from upstream demodulator corruption and generation stalls.

### 1. Synthetic TS Filler & PCR Keepalive During Upstream Stalls
- **Action**: When `tablo2hdhomerun` detects an upstream generation pause (> 2 seconds without a new HLS segment from Tablo), the proxy should not allow the downstream HTTP socket to go silent. Instead, it should inject **synthetic MPEG-TS null packets (`PID 0x1FFF`) with continuous Program Clock Reference (PCR) timestamps** on the video PID.
- **Expected Outcome**: Keeps the downstream TCP socket and Plex Ingest demuxer alive. Prevents Plex's read buffer from timing out while the Tablo tuner recovers or retunes.

### 2. In-Line Bitstream Sanitization & Corrupt Slice Gating
- **Action**: In [`MpegTsSync.scala`](file:///home/bdina/git/tablo2hdhomerun/src/main/scala/app/stream/MpegTsSync.scala), expand the packet gate to inspect the `transport_error_indicator` (TEI) flag and continuity counters. If a GOP contains corrupted sequence headers or widespread TEI errors, drop the damaged GOP and substitute null/filler packets until the next clean I-frame (`PUSI = 1` with valid sequence parameter sets).
- **Expected Outcome**: Shields Plex's FFmpeg decoder from ingesting corrupted bitstream syntax, completely eliminating the 7,800+ error flood that breaks downstream transcode pacing.

### 3. Transparent Watchdog Bridging Across Retunes
- **Action**: When an 8-second stall forces an upstream retune ([`ResilientHlsSource.scala:L122`](file:///home/bdina/git/tablo2hdhomerun/src/main/scala/app/stream/ResilientHlsSource.scala#L122)), maintain the downstream HTTP client connection transparently. Bridge the gap with synthetic null TS packets and rewrite incoming PTS/DTS timestamps on the new lease so Plex observes a single, uninterrupted live stream.
- **Expected Outcome**: Eliminates client-side stream crashes during upstream hardware recovery, matching the seamless playback experience of the official Tablo app.
