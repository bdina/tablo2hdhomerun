This document captures detailed timelines, cross-component accounting, and root-cause analyses for live TV streaming failures observed during playback. These empirical incident reports serve as architectural reference cases to drive resiliency, stream stability, and watchdog design improvements in `tablo2hdhomerun`.

## 2. Executive Summary of Incident

During a daytime viewing session of CBS 4.1 in windy conditions, the user experienced periodic stream dropouts. The Tablo tuner struggled to maintain a consistent signal lock due to the weather, causing the HLS stream to abruptly stop delivering data.

**The failure sequence was as follows:**
1. **Antenna Signal Drop**: The wind caused a sudden drop in signal quality at the antenna, preventing the Tablo from creating new video segments.
2. **Watchdog Intervention**: After exactly 8 seconds of no data, the `tablo2hdhomerun` watchdog correctly identified the stall and initiated a recovery tune sequence (`attempt=2`).
3. **Prolonged Tablo Recovery**: The Tablo device took a substantial amount of time (~18 seconds) to successfully re-acquire a signal lock and start returning a new HLS playlist.
4. **Client Impatience**: The combined 8-second detection delay and 18-second Tablo recovery delay resulted in a ~27-second video freeze. Around the 16-second mark of this freeze, the client (Plex or user intervention) timed out and restarted the stream.

**Conclusion:** 
The resilience logic within `ResilientHlsSource.scala` functioned exactly as designed, killing the stalled stream and attempting a reconnect. However, the physical delay of the Tablo tuner re-acquiring a lock during adverse weather exceeds typical client timeouts, resulting in the user having to manually return the stream or wait through a restart cycle.

## 4. Root Cause Technical Deep Dive

### The 27-Second Freeze

The core issue is a compounding of timeouts. When the wind causes the antenna signal to drop:

1. **Detection Delay (8 seconds)**: The proxy waits 8 seconds to ensure it's a true stream stall, not just a minor network jitter.
2. **Tablo Recovery Time (18-20 seconds)**: Unlike simply reading a file, a "retune" command forces the Tablo's internal ATSC 1.0 tuner to drop its current lock, scan the frequency, achieve a new lock, demodulate the stream, and spin up a new HLS packager. In windy conditions where multipath interference is high, this lock takes significantly longer than normal.

While the proxy attempts to keep the Plex connection alive by emitting empty MPEG-TS null packets (`GapFill`), the user is still staring at a frozen frame or loading spinner. Most user interfaces (and users themselves) have a patience threshold of 10-15 seconds. Because the total recovery takes nearly 30 seconds, the client abandons the stream before the auto-recovery can complete.

## 5. Architecture & Design Decisions

### Current State
The `ResilientHlsSource.scala` architecture is correctly managing its responsibilities. It detects failures, avoids crashing, and systematically attempts to recover the hardware tuner. 

### Future Considerations

**1. Aggressive Stall Detection:**
Currently, `stallTimeoutSec` is set to 8 seconds. If we lower this to 4 or 5 seconds, we can trigger the Tablo's recovery sequence sooner. While this risks false positives on heavily congested networks, it would shave several seconds off the total recovery time.

**2. Tune Timeout Adjustments:**
During a recovery phase, if the Tablo takes more than 15 seconds to return data, the client has likely already disconnected. We might consider adjusting `tuneTimeoutSec` to force another aggressive retry rather than waiting indefinitely for a lock that might never happen in bad weather.

**Current Decision:**
No immediate code changes are necessary, as the software is technically resilient and handling the hardware failure safely. However, adjusting `stallTimeoutSec` could be explored if wind-related dropouts become a common user pain point.
